# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Recruiters and hiring managers evaluating a full-stack developer candidate. Fellow competitive programmers and developers who discover the site through community links, GitHub, or social profiles. The user is looking at the portfolio on a laptop or desktop, often in a technical context, with a specific question: is this person worth talking to?

## Product Purpose

Personal portfolio and professional identity for Sumeet Kobarne, Full Stack Developer and Competitive Programmer from Pune. The site exists to make a strong first impression, communicate technical depth, and invite conversation. Success means a recruiter reaches out, a collaborator connects, or a visitor leaves with a clear sense of who Sumeet is and what he builds.

## Brand Personality

Bold, experimental, alive. The portfolio is not a resume in a browser; it is evidence of craft. Every interaction is intentional. The site should feel like it was built by someone who cares deeply about how things feel, not just how they work.

## Anti-references

- Generic Bootstrap/template sites: cookie-cutter layouts, forgettable structure, the "I followed a tutorial" look
- Minimalist to the point of empty: all-white, no personality, nothing to remember
- Neon cyberpunk overload: RGB everywhere, matrix rain, "hacker aesthetic" clichés
- Corporate SaaS landing pages: cold, conversion-optimized, metric-hero grids, feels like a product pitch not a person

## Design Principles

1. **The portfolio is the proof.** The craft of the site itself demonstrates the craft of the developer. No shortcuts; every detail is the work.
2. **Motion earns its place.** Animations exist because they make the content more alive, not because they look impressive. If removing an animation loses nothing, it should be removed.
3. **Bold without shouting.** Strong opinions expressed through restraint: one accent color, deliberate whitespace, type that does the heavy lifting.
4. **Substance over spectacle.** The visual system should surface the work, not compete with it. Personality lives in the details, not the volume.
5. **Alive means responsive.** Every interactive element should feel like it knows you're there. Hover states, cursor responses, scroll reveals — all of it is conversation.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Reduced motion support required for all animations. Color contrast at 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for large/bold text. Semantic HTML throughout.
